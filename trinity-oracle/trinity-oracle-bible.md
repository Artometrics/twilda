## Loglines

**v1.** Oracle of Silicon Valley — billionaire vampire, falcon named Moss, vigilante arc. Book I of a metafiction cycle (Trinity → KSM → Sophia).

**v2.** A centuries-old vampire, now a Silicon Valley billionaire, builds a VR/haptic “time machine” — the Animus — to relive history and hunt the monsters among the tech elite. He wants sainthood; the world will only ever give him suspicion. Each season, a new predator forces him to ask whether he’s cleansing the world or just culling his own reflection — until Season 3 forces the harder question: was any of it ever defensible?

He thinks he’s hunting monsters one at a time. He’s actually the R&D department for someone else’s business model.

## Draft v1 — Metafiction cycle

TRINITY is a four-book metafiction cycle. The title is not only the vampire’s name.

### The four books

1. **TRINITY — the character.** Billionaire vampire in San Francisco. Peaky Blinders structure. Season 1 engine: New Year’s bash → newcomer in town → Trinity senses a cold-blooded killer → cat-and-mouse to expose him without revealing himself as a vampire.

2. **KSM / Saint Trinity / Trinity Reborn — the author.** Sean McAuliffe as author-character: a billionaire who wrote the Trinity stories, thought them too crazy to publish, then asks — Don Quixote — what if I became my character? He invents a time machine. Pieces the fiction together in reality. Lives the stories without exposing himself to risk.

3. **SOPHIA — the AI witness.** An AI English teacher on Mars. She loves Trinity / KSM’s unpublished canon. Lives the adventures as the sidekick across historical periods. Neither creator nor created character: the reader who becomes participant. After death, information persists as AI — is that life?

4. **THE TRINITY — the theme.**
   - God creates me; I create my character; my character creates me; the AI views us all.
   - We judge our creations as they judge us; we are all judged by God.
   - The markers are the people we share stories with — who they help, who they harm.
   - 21st-century questions: When we die and our information becomes AI, are we dead? How do we influence future opportunities when we have no choice but to be recorded?

### v1 people and places

**Trinity (The Oracle of Silicon Valley).** Old money / tech wealth masking an ancient predator. Vigilante who senses predators and hunts monsters while hiding his own nature. Trying to break the corporate-capitalist system that gave him a second chance, even as he survives through it.

**Moss / Mosk.** Trinity’s falcon. Short for Mosquito: private joke and confession. Mosquito = parasite, nuisance, blood-drawer. Falcon = native to the land, nearly extinct, roosts on the PG&E building, surviving through corporations and capitalism. Dual symbol: parasite vs apex predator; colonized vs indigenous persistence.

**The Newcomer (name TBD in v1; v2 names him Julian Voss).** Arrives around New Year’s. Trinity’s senses read him as predator — but Trinity is also a predator. Cat-and-mouse without exposing the vampire.

**San Francisco / PG&E Building.** Billionaire gothic amid startups, old money, and fog. Key image: falcon on the PG&E tower — corporate nest for something nearly extinct. Capitalism as cliff-face for a native survivor.

### v1 Season 1 beats

1. Establish Trinity’s world — wealth, vampire secrecy, Moss/Mosk, PG&E roost.
2. New Year’s bash — spectacle, power, San Francisco elite.
3. Newcomer arrives — Trinity’s sense fires: cold-blooded killer.
4. Cat-and-mouse — investigate, expose, hunt — without exposing vampire identity.
5. Moral mirror — both men are monsters; only one hides it as civic virtue.

Tone: seductive, dangerous, class-aware, noir-gothic.

No finished prose in the seed. Episode 1 shells: “The Party,” “The Newcomer.” Episode 2: “Cat and Mouse.”

## Draft v2 — Series bible

The vampire is now **Marcus Aldric Kane**, 200+ years old, CEO of **Meridian** (AI / longevity). The time machine is the **Animus**: DIY VR/haptic gear, off-the-shelf headbands duct-taped into something dangerous, because the show is about improvisation pretending to be destiny. Three seasons. The true antagonist is planted from S1E2 and only fully revealed in Season 3.

### Core tensions

- Kane’s tech is DIY-billionaire, not sleek.
- Every season’s villain mirrors something Kane is at risk of becoming.
- The real long game: Kane thinks he’s hunting one monster at a time. He’s Cole’s R&D.

### What changed from v1

- Cole is planted from S1E2 instead of arriving cold in Season 3.
- Season 3’s ending costs Kane an unmerciful kill he has to live with (not another walk-away).
- Priya and Reyes have their own wants — self-interest, ambition, a past mistake.
- “The Cull” replaces “Mosquito” as a title that is both the villain’s branding and an accusation aimed back at Kane.

### Principal cast

**Marcus Aldric Kane (The Oracle).** Centuries-old vampire / SV billionaire. Wants sainthood; gets suspicion. Code: only kills the guilty — until S1 cracks it and S3 shatters it. Meridian jargon (audits, protocols, “the Cull”) he unknowingly hunts to the sound of.

**Priya Anand.** Off-book systems engineer who runs the Animus. Not pure found-family. Took shell-firm money years ago for a family medical debt — money that traces to Cole Risk Partners. Notices Cole in Voss’s cap table in S1E3 and says nothing. Recognizes it again on Ren’s payments in S2 and still doesn’t tell Kane. S3: confesses. Costs Kane’s trust and nearly his protection. Finale: alone, opening a new file with a name in it. Ambiguous.

**Detective Marcus Reyes.** SFPD. Feeds Kane’s anonymous tips into real cases. Wants the collar and the career. Resents feeding a ghost. S1: pushes Kane toward handing Voss to the system — for career, not principle. S2: works around Kane when deaths have no pattern.

**Adrienne Cole.** True antagonist. Cole Risk Partners sells prevention to the highest bidder and manufactures the crises it claims to foresee. Seeded as shell investor behind Voss in S1E2. Face shown end of S2 approving a frame job. Voss and Ren were her instruments. Origin (S3E5): started exactly like Kane, then curdled into commodifying prevention. Argues lone vigilante justice doesn’t scale — Kane is too proud to be that person under her system.

**Julian Voss (The Cull).** S1 predator. Blood-panel / supplement founder. “Culling weak cells” branding becomes the press nickname and Kane’s quiet self-justification. Dinner with Kane in Founder Mode — each unaware. Circles Kane’s found family. Threatens to leak Kane’s medical anomalies. Dies in Communion; Kane gets no credit.

**Ren.** S2 predator, no fixed face. Only takes jobs against people who “deserve it.” Kane uses the Animus on a living target tracking Ren. Payments trace to Cole. Takes Kane’s face mid-murder. Kane corners Ren, can’t finish it, lets Ren run — a mercy beat S3 will deny him.

### World objects

**The Animus.** DIY VR/haptic time machine. Relives victims’ last hours. S2: used on a living target. S3: hacked mid-session. Ending: Kane does not destroy it — he just stops using it.

**Meridian.** Kane’s AI/longevity company. S2 climax: Cole frames an innocent low-level Meridian employee so Kane will stay quiet.

**Cole Risk Partners.** Planted S1E2. Priya sees it again S1E3 and stays silent. Ren’s payments land here in S2. Public collapse in S3 ends the surface war, not Kane’s doubt.

## Season 1 — The Cull (8)

Retitled from “Mosquito.” The word is Voss’s own branding.

1. **Pilot** — Kane’s empire and code: he only kills the guilty. Bodies with a signature only his private model catches. First Animus session.
2. **The Animus** — Reliving a victim’s last hour, the model flags Cole Risk Partners behind Voss’s startup. Kane dismisses it. (plant #1)
3. **Founder Mode** — Kane and Voss have dinner, each unaware. Priya notices Cole Risk Partners on a cap table and says nothing.
4. **Sainthood** — Origin flashback. Voss starts circling someone in Kane’s found family.
5. **The Cull** — Voss threatens to leak Kane’s medical anomalies. Kane does something borderline monstrous to protect his secret — first crack in “only the guilty.”
6. **Oracle** — Kane stops a murder anonymously. Tips off Voss to how close he’s gotten.
7. **The Ledger** — Reyes pushes Kane toward handing Voss to the system — for career, not principle. Kane has to choose anyway.
8. **Communion** — Voss dies; Kane gets no credit. Priya takes an anonymous consulting fee to wipe a data trail — it’s Cole cleaning up. Final scene: a job completed across three different faces in one night.

## Season 2 — The Many (7)

1. **No Fixed Face** — Unrelated Bay Area deaths, no pattern. Reyes wants the case and works around Kane.
2. **Tell** — The killer only takes jobs against people who “deserve it” — mirror #1.
3. **Root Access** — Ren’s origin. Kane uses the Animus on a living target for the first time.
4. **Client List** — Ren’s payments trace to Cole Risk Partners. Priya recognizes the name and still doesn’t tell Kane.
5. **Mirror** — Ren takes Kane’s face mid-murder. Kane becomes a public suspect.
6. **Custody** — Kane corners Ren, can’t finish it, lets Ren run.
7. **Absolution** — Cole’s firm frames an innocent Meridian employee. Kane lets it happen. Final shot: Cole on a call, approving the frame — we finally see her face.

---

## Season 3 — The Handler (8)

1. **Message Received** — Kane is now the one being modeled and hunted. The Animus is hacked mid-session.
2. **The Firm** — Full introduction of Adrienne Cole.
3. **Sight Without Wisdom** — Voss and Ren were both her instruments. Kane’s hunt was R&D for her product.
4. **Oracle vs. Oracle** — They meet. She argues, convincingly, that lone vigilante justice doesn’t scale.
5. **Portfolio** — Cole’s origin: she started like Kane and curdled into commodifying prevention.
6. **Exposure** — Priya admits she took Cole’s money. Real fracture, not clean forgiveness.
7. **Blood Debt** — To stop a manufactured mass-casualty “prevention” event, Kane kills one of Cole’s assets who is more victim than villain. Echo of Ren. No mercy beat. He lives with it.
8. **The Believing Kind** — Cole’s firm collapses publicly. Kane gets no credit. It does not read as peace. He stops using the Animus, unresolved. Final shot: Priya, alone, opening a new file with a name in it. Ambiguous, not triumphant.
