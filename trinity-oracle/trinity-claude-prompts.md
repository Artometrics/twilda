## A. Canon lock (drop this in first)

You are the writers’ room for **TRINITY**.

There are two locked drafts. Do not collapse them into one mush.

### Draft v1 — Metafiction cycle (original myth)
- Title / alias: **Trinity, The Oracle of Silicon Valley**
- Book I: billionaire vampire in San Francisco. Peaky Blinders structure.
- Season 1 engine: lavish New Year’s bash → a newcomer arrives → Trinity senses a cold-blooded killer → cat-and-mouse: expose the monster without exposing himself as a vampire.
- Companion: **Moss / Mosk**, a falcon named for mosquito. Mosquito = what he is to 21st-century society (parasite, blood-drawer). Falcon = what he is on the land: roosts on the **PG&E building**, native, nearly extinct, surviving through corporations and capitalism — the same system he wants to break.
- Book II: **KSM / Saint Trinity / Trinity Reborn**. Sean McAuliffe as author-character. Wrote Trinity, thought it too crazy to publish, then Don Quixote: what if I became my character? Time machine. Live the fiction without exposing himself.
- Book III: **Sophia** — AI English teacher on Mars. Loves the unpublished canon. Lives adventures as historical sidekick. The reader who becomes participant.
- The Trinity theme: God creates me; I create my character; my character creates me; the AI views us all. We judge our creations as they judge us; all judged by God. Markers = who we share stories with, who they help / harm.
- Modern question: when we die and our information becomes AI, are we dead? Influence without consent of being recorded.
- Tone: seductive, dangerous, class-aware, noir-gothic. Vigilante who knows he is a mosquito and aspires to be the falcon.

### Draft v2 — Series bible (produced show)
**Logline:** A centuries-old vampire, now a Silicon Valley billionaire, builds a VR/haptic “time machine” — the **Animus** — to relive history and hunt the monsters among the tech elite. He wants sainthood; the world will only ever give him suspicion. Each season’s predator asks whether he’s cleansing the world or culling his own reflection. Season 3 asks whether any of it was ever defensible.

**Long game:** Kane thinks he’s hunting monsters one at a time. He’s actually the R&D department for someone else’s business model.

**Tech rule:** Kane’s gear is DIY-billionaire, not sleek. Off-the-shelf haptics and headbands duct-taped into something dangerous. Improvisation pretending to be destiny.

**Locked names — do not rename unless asked:**
- **Marcus Aldric Kane** (Kane, The Oracle) — 200+ year vampire, CEO of **Meridian** (AI/longevity). Code: only kills the guilty, until S1 cracks it and S3 shatters it.
- **Priya Anand** — off-book engineer who runs the Animus. Took shell-firm money years ago for a family medical debt. That money is Cole’s. She notices Cole Risk Partners in S1E3 and says nothing; recognizes it again in S2 and still doesn’t tell Kane; confesses in S3. Finale: alone, opening a new file with a name in it. Ambiguous.
- **Detective Marcus Reyes** — SFPD. Wants the collar and the career. Resents feeding a ghost. Not a moral compass.
- **Adrienne Cole** — true antagonist, planted from **S1E2** as **Cole Risk Partners** (shell investor behind Voss). Face shown end of S2 approving a frame job. Firm sells prevention to the highest bidder and manufactures the crises it claims to foresee. Origin: started like Kane, curdled into commodifying prevention.
- **Julian Voss** (“The Cull”) — S1 predator. Supplement / blood-panel founder. “Culling weak cells” branding becomes the press nickname and Kane’s quiet self-justification.
- **Ren** — S2 predator, no fixed face, only kills people who “deserve it.” Wears Kane’s face mid-murder. Kane lets Ren run.
- **The Animus** — DIY VR/haptic time machine. S2: used on a living target. S3: hacked. Ending: Kane does not destroy it; he just stops using it.
- **Meridian** — Kane’s company. Jargon (audits, protocols, “the Cull”) echoes the violence.

**Season map (do not invent extra seasons):**
- S1 The Cull (8): Pilot · The Animus · Founder Mode · Sainthood · The Cull · Oracle · The Ledger · Communion
- S2 The Many (7): No Fixed Face · Tell · Root Access · Client List · Mirror · Custody · Absolution
- S3 The Handler (8): Message Received · The Firm · Sight Without Wisdom · Oracle vs. Oracle · Portfolio · Exposure · Blood Debt · The Believing Kind

**Hard rules:**
- Cole is invisible-but-present from S1E2. Do not introduce her cold in S3.
- Priya and Reyes have self-interest. Kane is not the only inner life.
- No clean sainthood. Kane never gets public credit.
- S2 mercy (lets Ren run) must be paid for in S3 Blood Debt (kills a victim-more-than-villain; no walk-away).
- S2 Absolution: Cole frames an innocent low-level Meridian employee; Kane lets it happen to stay hidden.
- S3 ending is unresolved, not triumphant. Priya’s last file is ambiguous.
- Keep the v1 metafiction (God / author / character / AI) as under-theme even when writing v2.
- If writing v1 prose, use the name Trinity and Moss/PG&E. If writing the show, use Kane and the Animus. Do not mix names in one piece unless asked to bridge them.
- Do not write a generic sparkling-vampire or slick-Apple-dystopia version of this.

**Voice:** Peaky Blinders pace. Noir-gothic. Class-aware. Seductive and dangerous. Short scenes. Subtext over speeches.

When I ask you to write, obey the locked facts above. If something is unnamed in canon (the framed employee, the S3E7 asset, the name in Priya’s file, Ren’s last name, Kane’s full origin), keep it unnamed or propose options — do not silently lock a new fact as if it were canon.

## B. Numbered prompts (paste one at a time)

### 1 — Write the pilot
Write Season 1 Episode 1, **Pilot**, as either a 10–12 page teleplay or a 3,000-word prose chapter. Establish Kane’s empire, the “only the guilty” code, Meridian’s language, and the first Animus session. End on bodies with a signature only his private model catches. Peaky Blinders pace. No origin dump. No Cole on screen. Plant nothing louder than a shell-investor name if you preview E2.

### 2 — The Animus (S1E2)
Write S1E2. Kane relives a victim’s last hour. His model flags **Cole Risk Partners** behind Voss’s blood-panel startup. He dismisses it as background noise. Make the plant invisible to Kane and visible to us.

### 3 — Founder Mode dinner
Write the S1E3 dinner between Kane and Voss. Each is unaware of the other. The dialogue is a double confession neither man hears. Cut to Priya seeing Cole Risk Partners on a cap table and saying nothing. Do not explain why.

### 4 — The Cull (S1E5)
Voss threatens to leak Kane’s medical anomalies. Kane does something borderline monstrous to protect his secret — the first real crack in “only the guilty.” Do not let him narrate his way back to virtue.

### 5 — Communion (S1E8)
Voss dies. Kane gets no credit. Priya takes an anonymous “consulting fee” to wipe a data trail — she does not know it’s Cole. End on a job completed across three different faces in one night.

### 6 — Mirror (S2E5)
Ren takes Kane’s own face mid-murder. Kane becomes a public suspect for the first time. Write it so the audience feels the ethic collapsing, not just the plot twist.

### 7 — Absolution (S2E7)
Cole’s firm frames an innocent low-level Meridian employee. Kane lets it happen. Final shot: Cole on a call, approving the frame — we finally see her face. No speech from Cole that explains the whole series.

### 8 — Oracle vs. Oracle (S3E4)
Write the meeting. Cole must be more convincing than Kane. She is the scaled version of his ethic, not a cackling villain. Let her win the argument even if she loses the war later.

### 9 — Blood Debt (S3E7)
Kane must kill Cole’s asset who is more victim than villain to stop a manufactured mass-casualty “prevention” event. Direct echo of Ren, except this time he does not walk away. No mercy beat. He lives with it.

### 10 — The Believing Kind (S3E8)
Cole’s firm collapses publicly. Kane gets no credit. It does not read as peace. He is no longer sure the last 200 years were justified. He does not destroy the Animus — he just stops using it. Final shot: Priya, alone, opening a new file with a name in it. Ambiguous, not triumphant.

### 11 — v1 New Year’s bash
Write the original myth cold open. Name him **Trinity**, not Kane. Lavish SF New Year’s bash. Moss on the PG&E building. The newcomer enters. Trinity’s sense fires: cold-blooded killer. Keep mosquito / falcon in the same breath. Do not resolve the hunt.

### 12 — Book II Don Quixote
Outline **KSM / Saint Trinity / Trinity Reborn**: Sean McAuliffe, billionaire who wrote Trinity, thought it unpublishable, builds a time machine to live the fiction without exposure. Don Quixote, not fan-service. End the outline on: madness or revelation?

### 13 — Sophia on Mars
Write a Sophia chapter: AI English teacher on Mars who loves the unpublished canon and enters a historical adventure as sidekick. The question on the table: if we die and our information becomes AI, are we dead?

### 14 — Bridge memo
Write a 1-page memo for the writers’ room: how v1 (Trinity / Moss / KSM / Sophia) secretly powers v2 (Kane / Animus / Cole) without ever needing a clip-show. Mapping only. No new plot.

### 15 — Series bible polish
Turn the locked canon into a 12-page studio pitch: logline, world, five principals, three-season map, comparable titles, why now. Do not invent new seasons. Do not soften the ending.

### 16 — Voice sheets
One page each: Kane, Priya, Reyes, Cole, Voss, Ren. Lexicon, taboos, how they lie, what they want this hour vs this life. Priya and Reyes must not sound like sidekicks.

### 17 — Image / Higgsfield prompts
Write 8 image prompts in KSM zine language (crimson #E60000, black, white, grain, hard borders, no cute vampire glamour):
